# v4.2.0 - Standard test specimens and the zig-zag extrusion test

Week 10, 2026-09-10. Branch `feature/4.1.0-ldm-bricklayer-wave-infill`.

One component changes: **IAMLab Test Geometry**. It gains the flexural and
compressive specimens of the DIN EN and ASTM test standards, and a zig-zag
extrusion test path. Its icon is redrawn to match.

---

## 1. Standard test specimens

Until now the component made free shapes - cylinder, vase, frustum, wall, box -
sized from its own inputs. Fine for a shape study, useless for a mechanical test:
a printed block is not comparable to anyone else's printed block, and a testing
lab wants a specimen at a dimension it recognises.

Six standard bodies are added to the dropdown. Their dimensions come from the
standard, not from `height`/`radius`/`length`/`width`:

| Shape | Standard | Nominal (mm) | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| `BendingEN196` | DIN EN 196-1 | 160 × 40 × 40, span 100 | 3-point bending of cement mortar; the halves are then crushed between 40 × 40 mm platens |
| `BendingISO178` | DIN EN ISO 178 | 80 × 10 × 4, span 64 | 3-point bending of plastics, 16:1 span-to-depth |
| `BendingASTMD790` | ASTM D790 | 127 × 12.7 × 3.2, span 51.2 | 3-point bending of plastics, 16:1 span-to-depth |
| `CompressionEN12390Cube` | DIN EN 12390-1/-3 | 150 cube | compressive strength of hardened concrete |
| `CompressionEN12390Cylinder` | DIN EN 12390-1/-3 | Ø150 × 300 | compressive strength of hardened concrete |
| `CompressionASTMD695` | ASTM D695 | 12.7 × 12.7 × 25.4 | compression of rigid plastics, along the long axis |

The **bending span is reported alongside the dimensions**, because it is the
number the test rig is set to and it cannot be read off the specimen. The
concrete entries name two parts of EN 12390 on purpose: the shapes and
tolerances are in **-1**, the compression method in **-3**.

For a cement-based print, EN 196-1 is the closest fit of the six - it is the
reference method for cementitious binders, and one prism yields both the
flexural strength and, from its halves, the compressive strength.

### `specimenScale`, and why it has to exist

An ISO 178 bar is 4 mm thick and an ASTM D790 bar 3.2 mm. Both are **thinner
than a single LDM bead**, so at nominal size the slicer produces a wall or
nothing at all, silently. `specimenScale` scales the body uniformly - and the
span with it, so the rig still matches the specimen - and the component emits a
remark below 5 mm saying exactly that.

A scaled specimen draws a second remark: it is no longer at the dimensions the
standard specifies, so its results are comparable within your own series but not
to the standard.

### What is deliberately not claimed

These are **nominal geometries**. Nothing asserts that a printed body is a valid
specimen under the standard: layer orientation, surface finish, curing and the
anisotropy of an extruded part all sit outside the geometry, and every one of
these standards was written for cast or moulded material. The component and the
wiki both say so. Use them to make a series internally comparable and to speak
to a lab in its own dimensions - not to claim conformity.

## 2. The zig-zag extrusion test path

A boustrophedon line: straight passes joined alternately at each end, optionally
stacked. Inputs are the pass length, the pitch (set it to the bead spacing under
test), the number of passes, layers and layer height, and whether the 180°
reversals are rounded into a half circle of radius pitch/2 or turned on the spot.

**It is a toolpath, not a solid.** `geometry` is empty on purpose and the line
comes out of a new **`path`** output (index 3, appended), which goes straight
into the G-code generator - putting a curve through the slicer would try to
section a curve. The component says this in a remark and in `info`, because it is
the one thing a user will get wrong.

The reversals are the point of the test. A straight line only shows steady-state
flow; a turn shows whether the bead tears on the inside of the corner and
over-deposits on the outside. Stacked layers alternate their start side, so a
stack does not seam at the same end on every layer.

## 3. Where the logic lives

The dimensions and the path generator are **Rhino-free Core code**
(`IAMLabSlicer.Core/Calibration/TestSpecimens.cs`), not drawing code inside the
component. That is the difference between numbers that are unit-tested and
numbers that drift the next time someone edits a `switch`. The component is a
thin adapter that maps `Vec3` onto the base plane and turns a specimen into a
`Box` or a `Cylinder`.

`TestSpecimenTests` and `ZigzagPathTests` (34 cases) pin every nominal dimension
against its standard, assert the 16:1 span ratio of both plastics bars rather
than trusting two hard-coded numbers, and cover the path's geometry: pass
alternation, pitch spacing, centring, layer stacking and seam alternation, the
rounded turn's bulge past the end of the pass, and that every invalid option
throws instead of producing a degenerate path.

## 4. The icon

Redrawn from a vase to a **three-point bending rig** - specimen on two supports
under a load arrow - in the same Calibrate-group palette and the same
gradient-and-dark-outline language as the rest of the ribbon. The old icon
showed a vase, which is now one shape of thirteen and no longer what the
component is for. Source: `Resources/icons-src/IAMLab/IamTestGeo.svg`, which stays
the canonical definition.

**The raster was not produced by `make_icons.ps1` this time.** That pipeline
renders each SVG in headless Edge, and headless Edge hangs on this machine - it
spawns, never writes a screenshot and never exits, for the batch script and for a
single reduced invocation alike (16 stuck `msedge.exe` processes on the first
attempt). The 24x24 PNG was instead rendered from the same SVG geometry with
Pillow at 8x supersampling, downsampled LANCZOS, with the same
`drop-shadow(1px 1px 1px rgba(0,0,0,.30))` the pipeline's CSS applies, and
checked against the same assertions `make_icons.ps1` makes (24x24, 32-bit ARGB,
both visible and transparent pixels present). Re-running `make_icons.ps1` on a
machine with working headless Edge will regenerate it canonically and is the
right thing to do before release; the SVG is unchanged by this detour.

## 5. Compatibility

The `Shape` enum is **append-only** and nothing was reordered: the selection
persists as an integer, so a saved document keeps the shape it had.

The component's contract changes - two new input groups (`standard`, `zigzag`,
seven inputs) and the appended `path` output - are declared against the contract
baseline as `a1a2c3d4-0010-…0010:all`. Existing wires are unaffected: the new
output is appended and no existing socket moved.

The contract gate corroborates exactly that: the wired round-trip went from 374
to **375** default wires (the one appended output) and from 586 to **594**
expanded (the seven new inputs plus the output). Nothing else moved.
